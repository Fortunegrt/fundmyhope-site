A closer look at how FundMyHope works.

Practical habits for organizers and donors — the small things that make a fundraiser trustworthy.

For organizers

Getting the most out of your fundraiser

A fundraiser rarely spreads because of the platform it's on. It spreads because the story is clear and the first few people who see it trust it enough to share it.

1

Be specific, not just heartfelt

"Help my family" says less than "Help cover three weeks of hospital bills after my father's surgery." Specifics are what let a stranger picture the need and decide to help.

2

Set a goal you can explain

If someone asks "why that number," you should have a one-sentence answer. A goal that's clearly tied to a real cost is easier to trust than a round number.

3

Add one clear photo

It doesn't need to be professional. A real photo of the person, place, or situation does more than a stock image ever will.

4

Share it yourself, first

The platform doesn't send your page to strangers. The first wave of support almost always comes from people who already know you — send it directly before posting it anywhere public.

5

Post updates as things change

An update two weeks in — even a short one — tells donors their contribution went somewhere real. Campaigns that go quiet after the first day are the ones people stop trusting.

6

Thank people specifically

A general "thank you all" is fine, but where you can, thank people by name in your updates. It costs nothing and it's the biggest reason people give again.

For donors

Deciding what to support

You don't need to be an expert to give thoughtfully. A few habits go a long way.

Read past the headline

The full story usually tells you whether the need is specific and current, or vague and generic. Vague isn't necessarily dishonest, but specific is easier to trust.

Check for updates

A campaign that's been updated since it launched is a good sign the organizer is actually engaged with their supporters, not just collecting.

Look at the reviews

Reviews left on a campaign's page (from other donors or the organizer's own updates) can tell you more than the pitch itself. Start small if you're unsure, and give more once you've seen it followed through.
